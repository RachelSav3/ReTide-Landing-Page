Task 1 notes and Task 2 alpha test

Case: F Atlantic Edge Surf School, Lahinch, Co.Clare

Task 1:

Hunch (before AI)

I think there is definitely a market for this solution in Ireland, given the amount of outdoor group activities and the uncertainty of Irish weather I believe most small family run outdoor/activity type groups like kayaking, hiking, childrens sport clubs, beach yoga would invest in this technology for their business.

Niche

My chosen niche Small, independently owned kayaking and canoeing activity providers in Ireland that offer guided tours, group lessons and recreational experiences on rivers, lakes and coastal waters, where changing weather, water levels and tidal conditions regularly cause cancellations and rescheduling.

Buyer: The owner or manager of a small water activity business who would pay for the software to reduce administration, improve rebooking efficiency and protect revenue.

Daily user: The owner/ lead instructor/operations coordinator on the beach, lake or river managing real-time lesson logistics.

Invented Pain Line (Buyer): "I lose thousands of euros every season because when the swell blows out, I’m stuck on the beach for three hours cold-calling people while some of them just give up and ask for a refund."

Invented Pain Line (Daily User): "I can’t tap small buttons on a full desktop booking site using a wet smartphone screen with salty hands while standing in a cold wetsuit on a beach"

Technology Type

The proposed product is a mobile-friendly cancellation and rebooking management app.

It is not intended to replace a surf school's entire booking system. Instead, it focuses on managing weather-related cancellations, finding alternative sessions and identifying revenue at risk.

How the niche currently handles this

Based on the case, Niamh uses a paper diary, phone calls and WhatsApp to manage cancellations and rebook customers.

Other surf schools may use online booking software, spreadsheets or messaging apps. However, my proposed solution focuses specifically on making the cancellation and rebooking process faster and easier for small operators.

Business Model

Business Model: €199 set-up fee (includes initial account setup, roster import, and staff training) + €49 per month base subscription per beach location.

Reasoning (Assumption): A modest upfront onboarding fee secures operator commitment, while a low monthly subscription pricing model easily pays for itself by saving just two lost customer bookings a month (€90 value).

PRD

Lean Product Requirements Document (PRD)

Hypothesis: Small coastal activity operators will be interested in a simple, mobile-friendly app that reduces time spent managing weather-related cancellations and rebookings while protecting potential lost revenue.

Audience: Owners and managers of small, seasonal surf, kayaking, paddleboarding and coasteering businesses operating along Ireland's coast.

Call to Action: "Join the Early Access List" to register interest in trying the app.

Success Metric: At least 15% of targeted landing page visitors submit their contact details within the first month.

Out of Scope: Online payment processing, public-facing booking systems, customer refunds and staff payroll management.

ALPHA TEST

I opened ReTide on my phone to test how Niamh would use it while working on the beach. I checked the Today, Rebook and Money navigation buttons, the cancellation and rebooking features, and whether the interface was easy to use with one hand.

What broke or confused me:

Initially, none of the buttons responded when I tapped them on my phone. I also found it difficult to locate the beach conditions dashboard and the WhatsApp messaging feature.

I also wasn’t overly happy with the colour as I felt it might be difficult to read in bright outdoors conditions on a beach

What I changed:

I revised the app to improve the mobile navigation and make the features easier to find. I also changed the design to a brighter, high-contrast colour scheme, enlarged the navigation buttons and made them sticky so they remain visible while scrolling. I moved the testing to the published GitHub Pages website rather than relying on the HTML app preview.
